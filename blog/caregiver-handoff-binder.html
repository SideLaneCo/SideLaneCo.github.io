What to Put in a Caregiver Handoff Binder

Side Lane Co

At some point, someone else has to cover for you: a weekend away, a work trip, a sick day you can't avoid. The question that actually matters isn't whether you trust them. It's whether they'd know what you know, fast enough, without calling you every twenty minutes.

Why a quick roles sheet isn't enough

Most handoff templates out there are built for a short window: a few hours, an afternoon. They're a roles-and-contacts sheet, and that's genuinely all a short window needs. But overnight and multi-day coverage is a different problem: the person stepping in needs to actually run the routine, not just know who to call if something's wrong.

What a real overnight handoff needs

  • A walkthrough of the actual day, not just a task list. Morning, midday, evening: what happens, in what order, and what "normal" looks like at each point, so a deviation is easy to spot.
  • Medication instructions written for someone with zero context. Not "the usual dose," the actual dose, the actual time, what to do if a dose gets missed.
  • Real red flags, spelled out. What's a normal bad day versus what's actually an emergency. The person covering for you doesn't have your months of pattern-recognition, so write down what you'd notice automatically so they can notice it too.
  • Something they can actually read fast. If it takes 20 minutes to find the one instruction they need at 11pm, the binder isn't doing its job.
