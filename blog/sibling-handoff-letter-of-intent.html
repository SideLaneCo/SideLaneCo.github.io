The Document Special-Needs Planning Assumes Exists, and Usually Doesn't

Side Lane Co

Sometimes it's called a Letter of Intent. Every special-needs planning guide references one, the document that captures everything a parent or primary caregiver knows, so the next person doesn't have to start from zero. Most families don't actually have one. Not because they don't understand why it matters, but because nobody's built a version that's actually designed to be filled out, kept current, and handed off, rather than a scattered checklist buried in a nonprofit's PDF library.

Two people, two very different moments

A Letter of Intent gets written by someone who currently holds all the knowledge, and read by someone who doesn't yet, often a sibling, often years later, sometimes with no warning at all. That gap between when it's written and when it's actually needed is exactly why most attempts at this go stale or never get finished. It has to work as a document you write once and update yearly, not a project you mean to get to.

Why "sibling" changes what belongs on the page

Most future-care planning material is written for parents or guardians handing off to an institution or a professional caregiver. A sibling stepping in is a different situation entirely, they already know the person, they just don't know the logistics: where documents live, who's on the care team, what a good day looks like versus a bad one, what this person would want said on their behalf if they can't say it themselves. That's a different set of pages than a legal-guardianship checklist covers.

What actually needs to be on it

  • A Quick Reference page. The one page read first, in an emergency or otherwise.
  • Daily Life & Routine, written like a letter, not a form. The difference between a document that gets read and one that gets skimmed and set aside.
  • Questions the Sibling Will Have, answered ahead of time. Because the questions are predictable, even if the timing isn't.
  • A Review Log. A Letter of Intent that's five years old is only slightly better than not having one at all.

The Sibling Handoff Future Care & Letter of Intent Planner is 12 pages, written from real experience, not researched cold: Quick Reference, Full Profile (communication, sensory needs, what comfort and distress look like), Daily Life & Routine, Medical & Care Team, Legal & Financial Landscape (a map of what exists, not legal advice), Support Network, What Makes Them Them, Questions the Sibling Will Have, Sibling's Own Notes, and a yearly Review Log.
