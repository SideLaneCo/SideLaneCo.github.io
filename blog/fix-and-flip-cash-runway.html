The Number That Actually Predicts Whether a Flip Finishes

Side Lane Co

Most flips don't fail because the renovation was bad. They fail because the money ran out before the renovation was done. A budget-vs-actual spreadsheet tells you what you spent. It doesn't tell you whether you're going to make it to the finish line, and by the time a normal budget sheet flags a problem, you're usually already in it.

Budget-vs-actual answers the wrong question

Every rehab budget template does the same thing: line items down the side, budgeted versus actual, a total at the bottom. That's useful for knowing where the money went. It's useless for knowing where the money is going, specifically whether what's left of your financing will cover what's left of the job. Those are two different numbers, and only one of them tells you if you're about to run dry.

The one tab that actually matters

Remaining financing versus remaining cost, checked against each other, flagged red, yellow, or green. That's the whole idea. A shortfall shows up as a warning weeks before it becomes a crisis, instead of showing up as a phone call to a hard money lender three weeks before the project's supposed to be done. It's the same underlying insight behind RehabRunway, brought into spreadsheet form for anyone who wants a file, not a login.

Built from a real flip, not a template

This wasn't designed cold. It came out of a real fix-and-flip, financed with a line of credit, carrying real monthly costs (taxes, insurance, utilities, loan interest) while the work got done.
