What's Actually on the Medical Interpreter Certification Exam

Side Lane Co

If you're prepping for CCHI's CoreCHI exam (the foundational certification for healthcare interpreters, and a prerequisite most paths toward full CHI certification run through), the biggest mistake is treating it as a vocabulary test. It isn't. It's 100 multiple-choice questions in two hours (15 of them unscored, used by CCHI to test future questions), scored on a 300-600 scale with a 450 passing score — and it tests real judgment, not just terminology.

The five domains, and why they're not all vocabulary

  • Professional Responsibility and Ethics — what an interpreter is and isn't allowed to do in the room, conflicts of interest, scope of role.
  • Managing the Interpreting Encounter — the actual mechanics of running an interpreted session, not just translating words.
  • Healthcare Terminology — the part everyone studies for, and the part that's actually only one piece of the exam.
  • U.S. Health Systems — how the system itself works, insurance, structure, referrals — knowledge that has nothing to do with bilingual skill.
  • Cultural Responsiveness — navigating cultural context without overstepping into advocacy or advice the role doesn't call for.

Where people underprepare

Bilingual fluency gets people in the door, but it doesn't cover Professional Responsibility, U.S. Health Systems, or Cultural Responsiveness — three of the five domains that have nothing to do with language skill itself. Someone who's fluent and experienced in the room can still fail on the parts of the exam that test the profession's actual boundaries and structure, not the language.

What's worth knowing before you buy a study guide

This exam covers the written CoreCHI exam only — it doesn't cover the separate oral/performance exams CCHI also administers for full CHI certification. Any guide that claims to cover the whole certification pathway in one product is either padding or overselling.

The CoreCHI Exam Study Guide is built directly from CCHI's own Candidate's Examination Handbook, covers all 5 domains at their real weighting, and says plainly upfront that it's the written exam only — not a substitute for the performance-exam prep that's a separate track entirely.
