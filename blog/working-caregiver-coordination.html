How to Coordinate Care When You're Also Working Full-Time

Side Lane Co

If you're caregiving and working full-time, you're not the exception — you're most people. Nearly half of full-time workers (43%) are juggling caregiving for a loved one right now, up sharply from just a few years ago. If it feels like there's no real system built for your specific situation, that's because there usually isn't one.

Where the actual friction is

It's rarely the caregiving tasks themselves that cause the breakdown — it's the coordination. Appointments nobody wrote down. Medication changes that didn't make it to the person covering the evening shift. A work meeting that runs long because nobody flagged the 3pm pickup. The people who study this report caregivers spend an average of 24 hours a week just arranging, managing, and coordinating care, on top of the caregiving itself and the job.

That coordination tax is invisible until it isn't — and it's a real driver of burnout. 82% of working caregivers say it's affected their stress levels, and more than half have missed work outright because of it.

Why generic planners miss the actual problem

Most caregiver planners out there are built around one dependent and one caregiver, full stop. That's not most people's real situation. If you're caring for more than one person — a parent and a child, two aging relatives, whatever your actual mix is — a single-dependent planner just doesn't hold your real week. You end up cross-referencing three notebooks, which is exactly the coordination tax described above, just on paper instead of in your head.

What actually helps

  • One shared view, not five separate ones. If you're covering multiple people, you need to see all of their care needs against your actual work hours on one page — not flip between notebooks.
  • A real emergency sheet, filled out before you need it. Not when you're standing in an ER at 2am trying to remember a medication dose from memory.
  • A place for the handoff, not just the schedule. Whoever steps in for you — even for a few hours — needs the same information you're carrying in your head, written down somewhere they can actually find it.
