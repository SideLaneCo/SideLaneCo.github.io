How to Actually Study for the CPACC Exam

Side Lane Co

The CPACC (Certified Professional in Accessibility Core Competencies) is 100 multiple-choice questions in two hours, and it's the entry-level credential IAAP offers for accessibility professionals. The mistake most people make studying for it is treating all the material as equally important. It isn't. The exam is weighted, and studying blind to that weighting means spending real hours on material that's worth a fraction of what you think it is.

The real weighting

DomainWeight
Disabilities, Challenges, and Assistive Technologies40%
Accessibility and Universal Design40%
Standards, Laws, and Management Strategies20%

Two domains carry 80% of the exam between them. If your study time is split evenly across three domains (or worse, weighted toward whichever one has the most reading material), you're underinvesting in the two domains doing most of the work.

Where people actually lose points

The Standards/Laws domain is the one people over-study, because it feels the most like traditional test material: named laws, named standards, memorizable facts. It's worth 20%. The Disabilities/Assistive Technology and Universal Design domains are more conceptual and harder to cram, which is exactly why they need real study time, not less.

What actually helps

  • Study in proportion to the weighting, not in proportion to how much reading exists for each domain.
  • Practice questions that mirror the real format. 4 options, 1 correct answer, no trick "select all that apply."
  • A guide honest about its own scope. IAAP's blueprint is public. A study guide should follow it exactly, not pad itself with adjacent material that isn't actually tested.

The CPACC Study Guide is built directly from IAAP's published exam blueprint, weighted to match the real domain breakdown above, with practice questions in the same format as the real exam.
