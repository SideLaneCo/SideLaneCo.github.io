What's Actually on the ADS Exam

Side Lane Co

IAAP's Accessible Document Specialist (ADS) exam is the hands-on companion to CPACC and WAS, and IAAP is explicit that it's not for beginners: it expects one to three years of real experience creating and remediating accessible documents, not just awareness of what accessibility means. Knowledge of automated checking tools alone will not get you a passing score.

The real domain weighting

DomainWeight
Creating Electronic Documents25%
Remediating Electronic Documents25%
Auditing and Testing Document Accessibility25%
Document Accessibility Planning and Training15%
Policy Promotion, Advocacy, and Advising10%

Three domains tie at 25% each, creating, remediating, and testing, which together make up three-quarters of the exam. That's a different shape than CPACC or WAS, where one domain usually dominates. ADS study time genuinely needs to spread evenly across the top three, not concentrate on one.

Where people underprepare

The auditing and testing domain is the one most candidates shortchange, because it's tempting to treat a clean automated-checker report as proof a document is accessible. It isn't. An automated tool can confirm an alt attribute exists, it cannot judge whether the description is actually meaningful. The exam expects you to know exactly where automated testing stops and real manual testing, with an actual screen reader, has to start.

The trap in the wrong answers

A recurring ADS pattern: a remediation scenario with several plausible-sounding fixes, where the wrong answers patch the format currently in front of you (the flattened PDF) instead of the more durable fix (going back to the authoring source, if it's available). Knowing when to remediate in the final format versus the original source, and why one is more durable than the other, is exactly the kind of judgment call the exam is built to test.

The ADS Exam Study Guide covers all five domains at their real weighting, with 20 practice questions split to match, built from IAAP's own published Body of Knowledge for people who already have hands-on remediation experience and want real exam prep, not a beginner's introduction to accessibility.
